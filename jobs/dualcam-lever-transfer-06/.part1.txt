#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Stage 1 deterministic design script for job dualcam-lever-transfer-06.

Option B, the declared successor after the Option A wide fail: the
cam + linkage lever. Cam y stays EXACTLY as the -05 record: a
plate cam with a translating roller follower, audited at its
cap-limited base circle 195 mm, carried verbatim -- its readings
must reproduce the -05 record to the printed digit, and the script
fail-closes if they do not. Cam x becomes a plate cam driving an
oscillating roller follower: the roller rides the short arm (length
a) of a pivoted lever, the long arm (length b) drives the x slide
through a pin-in-slot. No search, no solver, no network: pure
standard library, deterministic.

What it does, in order:

  A. Validates the frozen -02 task
     (jobs/sixbar-gap-transfer-02/gap_transfer_nostops.motion.json)
     against an independent analytic reconstruction of the loop and
     its two clamped-cubic-spline timing runs -- fail-closed at
     1e-9 normalized units. The task file is read, never modified;
     its SHA-256 is recorded as the byte-identity witness.
  B. Carries cam y verbatim (-05 polar machinery, base 195 mm) and
     checks every printed reading against the embedded -05 record.
  C. Designs the cam-x lever chain analytically through the exact
     rocker map (the actor's requirement: synthesize through the
     lever geometry, never scale by lambda):
       phi(theta)   = asin(-x(theta)/b)
       phi'         = -x'/(b cos phi)
       phi''        = -x''/(b cos phi) + x'^2 sin(phi)/(b^2 cos^3 phi)
     with b = 130/sin(phi_max) fixed by the 260 mm stroke at the
     swing half-angle phi_max -- itself a SCANNED design variable,
     because the brief's first cut (30 deg) is arithmetically
     infeasible at the 30 deg pressure-angle target: at the level
     pin the extreme-creep asymptote EQUALS the swing half-angle
     and the interior worst angle exceeds the target at every
     pivot offset, converging to it only in the infinite-offset
     limit. The scan (20.0 to 30.0 deg, 0.5 deg grid) is emitted
     as the recorded Option B frontier; the declared design point
     is 25.0 deg (the knee of the lambda-vs-swing tradeoff, with
     a 5 deg creep margin against the target). Emits the
     scale-free design (see the pressure-angle convention below),
     the derived lambda window, and the lambda pick.
  D. Audits the picked design: pressure angle, parametric
     pitch-curve curvature, undercut gate, the lever's own output
     transmission angle, the force-closure/spring coefficients,
     the reflected-inertia and tolerance-amplification rows, the
     combined swept-envelope row (the critic's addition), the
     combined shaft-torque coefficient, and the one-sided junction
     table in BOTH slide space and roller space (the critic's
     task-space continuity requirement).
  E. Witness-validates everything numerically: the chain
     reproduction checks, the contact simulation on both cams
     (roller center against the offset profile polyline), the
     independent normal-direction pressure-angle cross-check, the
     parametric-vs-polar curvature cross-validation on cam y, the
     exact stop velocities at the slide (G3 audited after the
     lever, per the debate), and two-run determinism.

Conventions recorded up front (frozen in the JOB scorecard):

  - Physical frame: millimetres, origin at gap centre on the belt
    line, y up; 1 normalized unit = sqrt(75700) mm. Cam shaft at
    the origin. Lever pivot P = (L, e); short arm a to the roller
    center C = P + a (sin phi, cos phi); long arm b to the pin
    T = P - b (sin phi, cos phi); the slide axis is horizontal
    through the pin, so the pin-in-slot absorbs b(1-cos phi)
    = 29.3 mm of vertical travel at phi_max = 25 deg.
  - Pressure angle (oscillating roller follower, derived and
    machine-checked two ways): the contact normal at the roller is
    along the lever arm line, n proportional to
    P + a(1+phi') u(phi) with u = (sin phi, cos phi), and the
    roller velocity is along t = (cos phi, -sin phi), so
      cos(alpha) = |P.t| / |P + a(1+phi') u|.
    Because u.t = 0 the numerator is independent of phi'. Where
    the follower is stationary (the coordinate dwell arcs and the
    two process-stop instants: x' exactly 0) the pressure angle is
    undefined; by declared convention it reads 0 there. This
    formula reduces to tan(alpha) = |h'|/(r_base + h) for a
    translating follower (cam y) -- the script cross-checks it.
  - Pivot placement is a design output, not a hand choice (the
    actor's adopted requirement), under three declared rules:
      (i) swing half-angle: scanned on the declared 0.5 deg grid
          20.0-30.0 deg. The brief's first cut 30 deg is REFUTED
          by the scan's first row and recorded: at any pivot depth
          the infinite-offset asymptote of the extreme-creep
          pressure angle equals the swing half-angle, approached
          from ABOVE at both creeps, so a swing of 30 deg cannot
          meet a 30 deg target at any finite pivot offset. The
          declared design point is phi_max = 25.0 deg, chosen at
          the knee of the cap-lambda-vs-swing tradeoff (below 25
          deg each further degree buys under 0.3 lambda against
          roughly 12 mm more long arm and swept-envelope height)
          and carrying a 5 deg creep margin against the target.
          The scan table is recorded data; the design point is a
          declared constant with its derivation in the JOB, open
          to Jean's review, never a silent knob.
     (ii) pivot depth: e = -a cos(phi_max), the unique depth at
          which the roller centre is level with the cam shaft at
          BOTH swing extremes. It is exactly the depth that
          EQUALIZES the two extreme-creep pressure-angle
          asymptotes at the swing half-angle; with phi_max below
          the target this pins both creeps below the target. The
          script emits a three-row sensitivity witness
          (e/a = -0.85, -cos 25 deg, -0.95) recording what the
          neighbouring depths do at the 30 deg target.
     (iii) pivot offset L: bisected (scale-free, see below) and
          then quantized UP to the 0.1 grid -- the picked L/a is
          the smallest 0.1-grid value whose worst grid pressure
          angle is strictly below the 30 deg target, so the
          printed margin is real and not a bisection boundary
          artifact; the unquantized infimum is reported alongside
          as data.
  - Scale-free design: cos(alpha) depends only on the ratios
    L/a, e/a and on (phi, phi'), and the pitch curve scales with
    a. So the design (L/a, worst alpha, curvature in units of a)
    is computed ONCE, and lambda only sets the physical scale
    a = b/lambda. The major radius is then exactly
    r_max = a (L/a + sin(phi_max)) (the roller sits level with the
    shaft at the swing extreme), the size cap gives
    a <= 285/rhat_max, and the undercut gate gives
    a >= 18.75/rhat_rho_min. The lambda window is that interval
    scaled by b = 130/sin(phi_max); the pick is the smallest
    lambda on the declared 0.05 grid inside the window (the
    actor's pick rule: least force multiplication at the knee).
  - Pitch-curve curvature (parametric form; the polar form does
    not apply to an oscillating follower): with the cam-frame
    pitch curve X(theta) = R(-theta) C(theta),
    X' = R(-theta)(C' + J C), X'' = R(-theta)(C'' + 2 J C' - C)
    for J the clockwise quarter turn, and the signed curvature
    chosen so that kappa > 0 means the curvature centre is on the
    shaft side -- the SAME convention as the -05 polar form. The
    script cross-validates this against cam y's polar readings
    over the whole grid. Undercut gate: min positive rho
    >= 1.25 x roller radius = 18.75 mm; negative rho is
    manufacturing data (the inward offset there increases the
    profile radius).
  - Contact simulation: the working profile is the pitch curve
    offset inward by the roller radius along the analytic normal;
    the witness checks that every grid roller centre keeps
    distance >= roller radius - 0.01 mm from the offset polyline
    (local fine pass, point-to-segment) with the minimum attained
    at the matching angle, plus a coarse far-field pass. The
    0.01 mm allowance is the declared polyline discretization
    tolerance (the analytic sagitta at this grid is below
    0.002 mm). Declared handedness convention, recorded because
    the profile offset needs it: the cam shaft rotates
    COUNTERCLOCKWISE with advancing theta (the cam-frame
    construction R(-theta) throughout). The engineering gates
    (pressure angle, curvature, radii, the cap) are
    handedness-independent; the profile's handedness is a
    manufacturing datum recorded with this convention. A wrong-
    sense offset produces a mirrored curve that visibly cuts
    into the roller circle in the far-field pass -- which is
    exactly how the cam-y model error was caught at staging
    (min far-field 9.04 mm vs the 15 mm roller before the fix,
    15.0 mm after).
  - The pressure-angle convention is additionally cross-checked
    against the polyline itself: at sample angles the contact
    normal is re-derived from the simulated foot point and the
    angle to the analytic roller velocity must agree with the
    formula to 0.1 deg.
  - Unrated quantities: no operating speed, drive law, payload or
    motor is prescribed; v and a are per crank radian; capacity
    and speed remain unrated, as throughout case study B.
  - Declared envelope proposals (audit rows, never gates): slide
    block half-length 65 mm, half-height 25 mm; the y-slide is
    carried by the -05 layout assumption (fits the same
    footprint); the two cam plates are axially stacked on the
    one shaft (share the plan view).

Exit code 0: every internal fail-closed check passed. Verdicts
are DATA: an engineering-gate fail or an envelope exceedance is a
recorded result, never an error exit. Exit code 1: an internal
check failed (task validation, a chain witness, the cam-y carry,
the curvature cross-validation, or determinism).
"""
